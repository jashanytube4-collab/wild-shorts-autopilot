This frog *freezes solid* every winter, and comes back to life in spring.
Meet the *wood frog*, found as far north as *Alaska* and the Arctic Circle.
When winter comes, it doesn't burrow deep underground.
It just hides under leaves, and lets itself *freeze*.
Its heart stops beating. It stops breathing.
Up to around two thirds of the water in its body turns to *ice*.
To survive, it floods its cells with *sugar*, which works like antifreeze, protecting them from damage.
In Alaska, wood frogs can stay frozen for *months*, at temperatures down to around *minus 18*.
Then, when spring arrives, they thaw out.
Their hearts start beating again, and within hours, they hop away as if *nothing happened*.
Scientists study wood frogs hoping to learn how to freeze human *organs* for transplants.
A frozen frog, in suspended animation.
