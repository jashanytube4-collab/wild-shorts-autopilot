This eel has a *second set of jaws* inside its throat.
Meet the *moray eel*.
When it bites a fish, a second set of jaws, called *pharyngeal jaws*, shoots forward from its throat.
It grabs the prey and drags it down into the stomach.
It's just like the creature in the movie *Alien*.
Moray eels hide in holes in the reef, with only their heads poking out.
They constantly open and close their mouths.
It looks threatening, but they're actually *breathing*, pumping water over their gills.
The *giant moray* can grow up to around *three meters* long.
Some morays hunt together with *groupers*. [roving coral grouper | Plectropomus pessuliferus]
The grouper even shakes its head to signal the eel to join the hunt.
Morays are often curious around divers, but they can bite if they feel threatened.
Two sets of jaws, and a surprising partnership.
