Why do zebras have *stripes*?
Scientists argued about it for over a century.
Camouflage? Confusing lions? Keeping cool?
The strongest evidence points to something tiny: *flies*.
In one experiment, researchers dressed horses in *zebra-striped coats*.
Horseflies approached, but struggled to land on the stripes.
They seemed to get confused, flying past or bumping into them.
In Africa, biting flies can carry deadly diseases, so stripes may be a *bug shield*.
Every zebra's stripes are *unique*, like a fingerprint.
And underneath their fur, zebras have *dark* skin.
So technically, zebras are black with white stripes.
Foals learn their mother's pattern, voice and smell in their first days.
Zebras also sleep standing up, and the herd takes turns *keeping watch*.
Stylish and fly-proof.
