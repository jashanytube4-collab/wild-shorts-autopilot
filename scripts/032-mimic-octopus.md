title: The Octopus That Can Impersonate Other Animals
subject: mimic octopus | Thaumoctopus mimicus

This octopus can pretend to be a *completely different animal*.
Meet the *mimic octopus*, discovered off Indonesia in the late *1990s*.
Most octopuses camouflage to *hide*.
This one disguises itself as something *dangerous*.
Flattening its body and fluttering its arms, it becomes a venomous *lionfish*. [red lionfish | Pterois volitans]
Tucking six arms into a hole and waving the other two, it turns into a deadly *sea snake*. [banded sea krait | Laticauda colubrina]
Flat on the seafloor, swimming in waves, it's a poisonous *flatfish*.
And it seems to choose the disguise depending on *who's attacking*.
When a damselfish harassed one, it became a *sea snake*, one of the damselfish's predators.
All of this from an animal with *no bones*, and a body that's only about *60 centimeters* long.
Octopuses have about *500 million neurons*, most of them in their arms.
Each arm can taste, touch, and almost *think* for itself.
The ocean's best actor doesn't need a costume. It *becomes* one.
