title: The Bird That Seals Itself Inside A Tree
subject: great hornbill | Buceros bicornis
mood: curious
tags: hornbill, great hornbill, birds, india, rainforest

This bird *seals* itself inside a tree for months.
Meet the *great hornbill*.
When it's time to nest, the female climbs into a hollow tree.
Then, using mud, food and her own droppings, she seals the entrance, leaving only a narrow *slit*.
She stays inside for weeks or months, laying eggs and raising the chicks.
She even *molts* her feathers, so she can't fly.
Her partner feeds her through the slit, bringing *fruit*, again and again.
If something happens to him, the whole family is in danger.
That big yellow helmet on its head is called a *casque*, and it's mostly hollow.
Great hornbills can live for around *50 years*.
They help forests grow by spreading the seeds of the fruit they eat.
In India and Southeast Asia, some communities consider them *sacred*.
