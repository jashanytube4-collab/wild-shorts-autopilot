This tiny animal isn't a shrew, and it's related to *elephants*.
Meet the *elephant shrew*, also called a *sengi*.
It got its name from its long, wiggly, trunk-like *nose*.
It uses that nose to sniff out insects in leaf litter.
Sengis are incredibly fast, reaching around *28 kilometers per hour*.
They build and keep a network of trails through their territory, clearing away leaves and twigs.
Then they race along them, escaping predators at top speed.
Many sengis pair up for life with one partner.
But they mostly live separately, meeting only occasionally.
In 2020, scientists rediscovered the *Somali sengi*, which hadn't been seen by scientists for around *50 years*.
DNA studies showed sengis belong to a group of African mammals that includes elephants, aardvarks and manatees.
A pocket-sized speedster, with an elephant's family tree.
