title: The Orangutan That Treated Its Own Wound
subject: Sumatran orangutan | Pongo abelii
mood: curious
tags: orangutan, smart animals, medicine, sumatra, apes
---
In 2022, scientists watched an orangutan *treat its own wound*.
A male named *Rakus* had a fresh gash on his face.
He chewed the leaves of a plant used in local medicine for pain and inflammation.
Then he carefully spread the juice onto his wound, and pressed the chewed leaves on top like a *bandage*.
Within days, the wound closed. A month later, it had *healed*.
It was the first time a wild animal was seen treating a wound with a *medicinal plant*.
Orangutans spend almost their whole lives in the trees.
Their arms can stretch over *two meters* wide.
Young orangutans stay with their mothers for around *seven or eight years*, learning which plants to eat.
Some make leaf umbrellas when it rains, and use leaves like *gloves*.
Sadly, their rainforest is being cleared, and all three species are *critically endangered*.
Their name means *person of the forest*.
