title: Alligators Can Survive Being Frozen In Ice
subject: American alligator | Alligator mississippiensis
mood: curious

When the water freezes, some alligators stick their *snouts* out of the ice.
In winter, in places like North Carolina, alligators have been filmed frozen into ponds, with just their noses poking through.
It's called *icing*.
By keeping their nostrils above the surface, they can keep breathing while their bodies go into a kind of *slow motion*.
When the ice melts, they swim away.
The *American alligator* has been around in a similar form for millions of years.
It can grow over *four meters* long.
And its jaws can bite with enormous force, but the muscles that open them are weak.
A person can hold an alligator's mouth shut with their *hands*, though it's a very bad idea.
The temperature of the nest decides if babies are *male* or *female*.
Alligators can live over *70 years*.
In the 1960s, they were endangered. Today, there are millions of them in the southern US.
