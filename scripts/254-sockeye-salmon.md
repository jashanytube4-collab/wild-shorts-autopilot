title: Why Salmon Swim Upstream To Die
subject: sockeye salmon | Oncorhynchus nerka
mood: epic
tags: salmon run, sockeye salmon, migration, alaska, fish
---
These fish swim *hundreds of kilometers upstream*, then die.
Meet the *sockeye salmon*.
They're born in freshwater rivers and lakes.
Then they swim out to the ocean, where they spend a few years growing.
And then, they come *home*.
Salmon find their way back to the exact stream where they were born, using Earth's *magnetic field* and their sense of smell.
They swim against the current, leaping up waterfalls, and dodging bears and eagles. [brown bear | Ursus arctos]
During the journey, they stop eating.
Their bodies turn bright *red*, and males grow humped backs and hooked jaws.
After laying and fertilizing their eggs, almost all of them *die*.
But their bodies feed the rivers, bears, birds, and even the *trees*.
In Bristol Bay, Alaska, tens of millions of sockeye salmon return every summer.
A journey home, and a final gift.
