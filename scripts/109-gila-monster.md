This lizard's venom helped create a *diabetes medicine*.
Meet the *Gila monster*, one of the few venomous lizards in the world.
It lives in the deserts of the southwestern US and Mexico.
Unlike snakes, it doesn't inject venom with fangs.
It bites down hard, holds on, and *chews*, letting venom flow into the wound along grooves in its teeth.
The bite is extremely painful, but rarely deadly to humans.
In its venom, scientists discovered a hormone-like substance.
They turned a version of it into a drug that helps people with *type 2 diabetes* control their blood sugar.
That research also helped lead to some of the weight-loss drugs used today.
Gila monsters eat only a few times a year, mostly eggs and baby animals.
They store fat in their thick *tails*.
They spend over *90 percent* of their lives underground.
A slow, beaded monster that might just help save lives.
