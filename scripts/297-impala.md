title: The Antelope That Leaps 10 Meters
subject: impala | Aepyceros melampus
mood: epic
tags: impala, antelope, africa, jumping, wildlife
---
An impala can leap around *ten meters* in a single bound.
And jump about *three meters* high.
When predators attack, a herd of impalas explodes in all directions, leaping high into the air.
The chaos makes it hard for lions or cheetahs to focus on just one. [lion | Panthera leo]
Impalas also have a clever trick.
They release scent from glands on their back legs as they leap, possibly to help the herd stay together.
They're one of the most common antelopes in Africa.
Male impalas have beautiful, curved, ridged *horns*.
During the mating season, males fight, roar, and chase each other all day long.
Impalas also groom each other, taking turns to clean ticks off.
Sometimes they're helped by oxpecker birds that eat ticks off their fur. [red-billed oxpecker | Buphagus erythrorynchus]
Impalas can give birth at midday, when predators are resting.
Grace, speed, and incredible jumps.
