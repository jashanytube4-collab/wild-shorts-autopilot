In this species, it's the *dad* who gets pregnant.
Meet the *seahorse*.
The female lays her eggs into a pouch on the male's belly.
He fertilizes them, and carries them for weeks.
Then he goes into labor, pumping his body to push out hundreds, sometimes over a *thousand* babies.
Seahorses are *fish*, even though they don't look like it.
They swim upright, using a tiny fin on their back that beats up to *50 times* a second.
They're terrible swimmers, so they hold onto seaweed with their curling tails.
They have no stomach, so they eat almost constantly.
Their eyes move independently, and they can change color to blend in.
Many seahorse couples greet each other every morning with a little *dance*, changing colors together.
Their scientific name, *Hippocampus*, means horse sea monster in Greek.
The part of your brain that controls memory is named after it, because of its shape.
