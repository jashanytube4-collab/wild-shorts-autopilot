title: The Insect That Looks Like A Flower
subject: orchid mantis | Hymenopus coronatus

This isn't a flower. It's a *predator*.
Meet the *orchid mantis*, from the rainforests of Southeast Asia.
Its legs are shaped like flower *petals*, and its body is pink and white.
For years, scientists thought it hid among real orchids to ambush prey.
But they discovered something even sneakier.
It doesn't need to hide among flowers at all.
Bees and butterflies are attracted to the mantis *itself*, as if it were a real flower. [western honey bee | Apis mellifera]
In one study, insects actually approached the mantis *more often* than real flowers.
When they land, it strikes with spiked front legs in a fraction of a second.
It's one of the few known cases of an animal pretending to be a flower to *lure* prey.
Females can be around *twice the size* of males.
And like other mantises, it can turn its head almost all the way around to watch you.
Beautiful, and deadly.
