This goose flies over the *Himalayas*.
Meet the *bar-headed goose*.
Every year, it migrates between Central Asia and India, crossing some of the highest mountains on Earth.
It can fly at altitudes of over *6,000 meters*, sometimes even higher.
At that height, the air has less than half the oxygen at sea level.
Most animals would struggle to breathe, let alone fly.
So how does it survive?
Its lungs are extra efficient, its blood holds onto oxygen better, and its heart pumps more blood to the muscles.
Some bar-headed geese have been tracked crossing the mountains in a single day.
Instead of flying high all the time, they often follow the shape of the land, climbing and dropping.
There are old stories of them being seen near the summit of *Mount Everest*.
Those claims are hard to prove.
But even without them, it's one of the highest-flying birds on Earth.
