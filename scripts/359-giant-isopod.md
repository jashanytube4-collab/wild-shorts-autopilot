title: The Giant Bug That Didn't Eat For 5 Years

In Japan, an aquarium's giant isopod went over *five years* without eating.
This is a *giant isopod*, a deep-sea relative of the tiny roly-polies in your garden. [common pill woodlouse | Armadillidium vulgare]
But this one can grow up to around *50 centimeters* long.
It lives in the cold, dark depths of the ocean, hundreds of meters down.
There, food is rare.
So giant isopods eat whatever sinks from above: dead fish, whales, and squid.
When they find food, they eat as much as they can.
Then they can survive for a very long time without eating again.
The isopod in Japan refused food from 2009 until it died in 2014.
Scientists still aren't completely sure why.
Giant isopods have huge eyes, with thousands of tiny lenses, which reflect light back.
They can roll into a ball to protect themselves, just like their garden cousins.
A deep-sea giant that knows how to wait.
