This beetle defends itself by shooting *boiling chemicals*.
Meet the *bombardier beetle*.
Inside its abdomen, it stores two separate chemicals in different chambers.
When threatened, it mixes them in a special reaction chamber.
They react *explosively*, heating to around *100 degrees Celsius*.
Then it fires a hot, toxic spray out of its rear, with a *popping* sound.
It can even aim, twisting its abdomen to spray in almost any direction.
The spray fires in tiny pulses, hundreds a second, which keeps the beetle from burning itself.
Scientists have studied it to design better fuel injectors and fire extinguishers.
In one study, toads swallowed bombardier beetles, and the beetles sprayed inside their stomachs. [Japanese common toad | Bufo japonicus]
Many of the toads threw them back up, and the beetles walked away alive.
A tiny chemical weapon, with perfect aim.
