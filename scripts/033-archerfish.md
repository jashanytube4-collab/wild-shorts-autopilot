This fish hunts insects by shooting them with *water*.
Meet the *archerfish*.
It hides under leaves in mangrove swamps, watching for bugs above the surface.
When it spots one, it presses its tongue against a groove in its mouth and fires a powerful jet of water.
The bug gets knocked off its leaf and drops straight into the fish's *mouth*.
It can hit targets up to *two meters* away.
And here's the crazy part: when light enters water, it bends.
So the fish is aiming from below, looking through a distorted surface, and it still *corrects for it*.
Some archerfish can even *jump* out of the water to snatch prey.
Scientists found they can learn to recognize *human faces*, too.
In one experiment, they spat at the right face from a lineup with up to *80 percent* accuracy.
Not bad for a fish whose brain is smaller than a pea.
