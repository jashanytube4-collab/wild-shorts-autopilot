One of the longest animals ever recorded was a *jellyfish*.
Meet the *lion's mane jellyfish*.
One washed up in Massachusetts in 1870 with tentacles reported to be around *37 meters* long.
That's longer than a *blue whale*.
Its bell can be over *two meters* across.
It has hundreds of long, sticky tentacles that hang down like a lion's *mane*.
They're covered in stinging cells that catch fish, shrimp, and other jellyfish.
Its sting is painful, but rarely dangerous to humans.
Even a dead lion's mane, or a broken-off tentacle, can still *sting*.
Lion's mane jellyfish live in cold waters of the Arctic, North Atlantic and North Pacific.
Small fish often hide among their tentacles for protection.
In a *Sherlock Holmes* story, a lion's mane jellyfish turns out to be the *culprit* behind a mysterious death.
A giant drifting mane of the cold seas.
