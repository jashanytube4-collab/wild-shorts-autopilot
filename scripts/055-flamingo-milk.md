title: Why Flamingos Are Pink
subject: greater flamingo | Phoenicopterus roseus
mood: curious
tags: flamingo, pink birds, crop milk, birds, wildlife
---
Flamingos aren't born *pink*.
Chicks hatch with grey or white feathers.
So where does the color come from?
Their food.
Flamingos eat *algae*, tiny shrimp, and other creatures full of natural pigments called *carotenoids*.
Those pigments turn their feathers pink, orange, or even *red*.
In zoos, if their diet isn't right, flamingos can fade almost to *white*.
They eat upside down, sweeping their heads through water and filtering food through their bills.
And then there's the milk.
Flamingo parents feed their chicks a red liquid from their throats called *crop milk*.
It's so red that it once looked like they were feeding them *blood*.
Both parents make it, mom and dad.
And while they do, they lose their own color, turning pale for a while.
As for standing on one leg?
Scientists believe it may actually save energy, because it takes less effort to stay balanced that way.
