This might be the *rarest big cat* on Earth.
Meet the *Amur leopard*, from the forests of the Russian Far East and northeast China.
In the early 2000s, there may have been only around *30* left in the wild.
Its thick, pale coat can grow over *seven centimeters* long in winter.
And its long legs help it walk through deep snow.
Amur leopards can leap over *five meters* horizontally.
They hunt deer, hares, and wild boar.
They're solitary, and mostly active at night.
Poaching, logging and loss of prey pushed them to the brink.
But thanks to a national park in Russia, and anti-poaching patrols, numbers have grown to over *100*.
Camera traps help scientists identify each leopard by its unique spot pattern.
It's still *critically endangered*.
A snowy forest ghost, slowly coming back.
