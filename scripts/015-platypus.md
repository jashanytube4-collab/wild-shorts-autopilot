When scientists first saw this animal, they thought it was a *hoax*.
In 1799, a dried platypus arrived in Britain, and experts checked it for *stitches*, convinced someone had sewn a duck's bill onto a beaver.
But the *platypus* is real, and it's even weirder than it looks.
It's a mammal that *lays eggs*.
It feeds its babies milk, but it has no nipples. The milk seeps out through its *skin*.
Males carry *venomous spurs* on their back legs, and the pain from a sting can last for *weeks*.
When it dives, it shuts its eyes, ears and nose.
So it hunts using its bill, which can sense the tiny *electric signals* of moving prey.
It doesn't even have a *stomach*.
And in 2020, scientists discovered that under *ultraviolet light*, its fur glows *blue-green*.
Nature clearly had some leftover parts that day.
