title: Why Cats Always Land On Their Feet
subject: domestic cat | Felis catus
mood: curious

Why do cats almost always land on their *feet*?
It's called the *righting reflex*.
When a cat falls, it uses its inner ear to figure out which way is *up*.
Then it twists the front half of its body first, tucking its front legs in to spin faster.
Then it twists the back half.
In less than a second, it's facing the ground.
Cats also spread their legs out like a little *parachute*, to slow down.
Kittens start learning this when they're around *three weeks* old.
Cats have a flexible spine and no fixed collarbone, which helps them twist.
In the 1800s, scientists were so puzzled by this that they filmed falling cats with early cameras.
It seemed to break the laws of physics, but it doesn't.
Strangely, cats falling from higher floors sometimes have *fewer* injuries than from mid-height.
They may have more time to relax and spread out.
But no, they don't always land safely. Keep windows closed.
