This insect spends *17 years* underground, then comes out all at once.
These are *periodical cicadas* from North America.
As nymphs, they live in the soil, sucking sap from tree roots.
Then, after 13 or 17 years, depending on the brood, billions of them emerge in the same few weeks.
Why such strange numbers? Both 13 and 17 are *prime numbers*.
Scientists think that makes it harder for predators with shorter life cycles to sync up with them.
And by emerging in such huge numbers, they *overwhelm* predators.
Birds and other animals get so full they can't eat them all.
Males sing together in choruses that can be as loud as a *lawnmower*.
After mating, they lay eggs in tree branches, and the adults die within weeks.
The babies drop to the ground and burrow down to start the cycle again.
In 2024, two broods emerged together for the first time since *1803*.
