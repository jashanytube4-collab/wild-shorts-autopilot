title: The Spider That Spins Golden Silk
subject: golden silk orb-weaver | Trichonephila clavipes

This spider spins silk that shines like *gold*.
Meet the *golden silk orb-weaver*.
Its webs can stretch over *a meter* wide, and in sunlight, the silk glows *golden yellow*.
Scientists think the color might attract bees or help hide the web in the sun.
Spider silk is incredibly strong, stronger than *steel* of the same thickness.
In 2009, a cape was made from the silk of over *one million* golden orb-weavers in Madagascar.
It took years to make, and it's now displayed in museums.
These spiders are large, with females growing several centimeters long.
Males are *tiny*, often hundreds of times lighter.
They often hang around the edge of the female's web, waiting for a chance to mate.
Their bite is mild, about like a bee sting.
Some golden orb-weavers are big enough to catch small *birds* in their webs.
A natural artist, spinning gold.
