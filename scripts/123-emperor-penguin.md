These birds raise their chicks in the *coldest winter* on Earth.
Meet the *emperor penguin*, the largest penguin in the world.
In the Antarctic winter, temperatures can drop below *minus 40*, with winds of around *200 kilometers per hour*.
While the females go to sea to feed, the males stay behind to keep the eggs warm.
Each male balances a single egg on his feet, under a warm flap of skin.
For around *two months*, he doesn't eat at all.
He can lose nearly half his body weight.
To survive, thousands of males huddle together, taking turns moving from the freezing outer edge to the *warm center*.
When the females return, the chicks are hatching.
Emperor penguins can dive deeper than *500 meters*, and hold their breath for over *20 minutes*.
They are the only penguins that breed during the Antarctic *winter*.
Toughness, on two short legs.
