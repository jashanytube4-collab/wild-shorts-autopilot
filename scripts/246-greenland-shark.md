Some sharks alive today might have been born before *Shakespeare*.
Meet the *Greenland shark*.
In 2016, scientists estimated that one female was around *400 years* old.
That makes it the longest-living animal with a backbone known to science.
Greenland sharks live in the freezing waters of the Arctic and North Atlantic.
They grow incredibly slowly, around *one centimeter* a year.
And they may not have babies until they're around *150*.
They're also among the slowest-swimming fish for their size, cruising at around *one kilometer per hour*.
Yet scientists have found *seals*, and even parts of *reindeer* and a polar bear, in their stomachs.
Many have tiny parasites attached to their *eyes*, which may leave them nearly blind.
Their flesh is toxic when fresh.
But in Iceland, it's fermented for months to make a dish called *hákarl*, famous for its smell.
An ancient, slow giant from the deep.
