title: The Songbird That Impales Its Prey
subject: loggerhead shrike | Lanius ludovicianus

This little songbird *impales* its prey on thorns.
Meet the *loggerhead shrike*, also called the *butcher bird*.
It's only about the size of a robin.
But it hunts insects, lizards, mice, and even small birds.
Shrikes don't have strong talons like hawks.
So they use thorns and barbed wire as a *meat hook*, sticking prey onto them.
That helps them tear food into pieces.
They also store food this way for later, like a natural pantry.
Males with bigger collections of stored prey may attract more *females*.
Loggerhead shrikes can even eat toxic grasshoppers.
They leave them impaled for a few days until the poison *breaks down*.
Shrikes perch on wires and fence posts, watching for movement below, then swoop down to strike.
They kill with a quick bite to the neck, using a special notch on their beak.
A cute bird with a grisly hobby.
