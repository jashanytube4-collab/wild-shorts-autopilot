title: How Wolves Changed Rivers
subject: gray wolf | Canis lupus
mood: epic
tags: wolves, yellowstone, trophic cascade, gray wolf, ecosystem
---
When wolves returned to Yellowstone, scientists say even the *rivers* began to change.
By the 1920s, wolves had been hunted out of *Yellowstone National Park*.
Without them, elk herds grew huge, eating young trees along the riverbanks. [elk | Cervus canadensis]
In *1995*, wolves were brought back.
Elk began avoiding open valleys where they could be ambushed.
Willows and aspens began to recover.
With more trees, *beavers* came back and built dams. [North American beaver | Castor canadensis]
Those dams created ponds that helped *fish*, *frogs* and birds.
This chain reaction is called a *trophic cascade*.
Scientists still debate exactly how much came from the wolves.
But it showed how one predator can affect a whole *ecosystem*.
Wolves themselves are incredible.
They can travel *dozens of kilometers* a day, and a pack's howl can carry for kilometers.
And every pet dog alive today is descended from ancient *wolves*.
