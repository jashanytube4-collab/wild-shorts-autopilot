Ancient Roman doctors used this fish to treat *headaches*.
Meet the *electric ray*.
It can produce electric shocks of up to around *200 volts* for some species.
In ancient Rome, a doctor named *Scribonius Largus* recommended placing a live electric ray on a patient's head.
The shock was supposed to numb the pain.
It's one of the earliest known uses of *electric therapy*.
The electric ray has two kidney-shaped organs in its body, made of stacked cells that work like batteries.
It uses them to stun fish and crabs, then wraps its body around them.
Its old name, *torpedo*, comes from a Latin word meaning *numbness*.
And yes, that's where naval torpedoes got their name.
Electric rays often hide buried in the sand, waiting for prey.
Step on one, and you'll feel it.
A living battery, from ancient medicine to modern weapons.
