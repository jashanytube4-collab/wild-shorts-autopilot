These ants turn themselves into living *honey jars*.
Meet the *honeypot ants*.
Some workers, called *repletes*, are fed so much nectar and sugary liquid by other ants that their abdomens swell up like *grapes*.
They become so big they can barely move.
So they hang from the ceiling of underground chambers, storing food for the colony.
When food is scarce in the dry season, other ants tap them, and the repletes *throw up* the stored liquid to share.
Honeypot ants live in deserts in North America, Africa and *Australia*.
For Aboriginal Australians, they're a traditional sweet treat.
People dig them up and bite off the sweet, honey-filled abdomens.
Rival colonies sometimes raid each other to steal the repletes.
Winners carry them home, alive.
