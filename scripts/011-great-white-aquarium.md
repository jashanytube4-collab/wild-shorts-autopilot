There's a reason you'll *never* see a great white shark in an aquarium.
And it's not for lack of trying.
For decades, aquariums tried to keep them in captivity.
Most of those sharks died within *days*, or stopped eating completely.
One in Japan survived just *three days* in 2016.
The record belongs to Monterey Bay Aquarium in California, which kept a young great white for *198 days* before releasing it back into the ocean.
So why is it so hard?
Great whites are built to travel.
One shark, named *Nicole*, swam from South Africa to Australia and back, over *20,000 kilometers*, in under a year.
In a tank, they repeatedly bump into the walls, get stressed, and refuse food.
Their snouts are packed with sensors that detect *electric fields*, and scientists think tanks may confuse them.
For a great white, the whole ocean isn't just home. It's a *hallway*.
