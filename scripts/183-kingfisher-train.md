This little bird helped redesign Japan's famous *bullet train*.
In the 1990s, Japan's bullet trains had a problem.
When they shot out of tunnels at high speed, they made a loud *boom*, disturbing people nearby.
An engineer, who was also a birdwatcher, thought of the *kingfisher*.
It dives from the air into water with barely a *splash*, thanks to its long, pointed beak.
So engineers reshaped the train's nose to be more like a kingfisher's beak.
The new trains were quieter, faster, and used less *electricity*.
The common kingfisher dives at speed to catch fish, then beats them on a branch before swallowing them head first.
It has special eyes that can adjust for the change in light between *air and water*.
And its bright blue color isn't from pigment. It comes from the way its feathers *reflect light*.
Nature solved the problem first.
