title: Ants That Sew Leaves Together With Silk
subject: weaver ant | Oecophylla smaragdina
mood: curious

These ants build homes by *sewing* leaves together.
Meet the *weaver ants*.
First, a team of workers grabs the edges of two leaves.
They form living *chains*, holding onto each other to pull the leaves closer.
Then other workers bring in their own *larvae*.
They gently squeeze them, and the larvae release *silk*.
The workers move them back and forth like living glue guns, stitching the leaves together.
A single colony can have hundreds of thousands of ants, spread over many trees.
Weaver ants are fierce, and attack intruders by biting and spraying *acid* into the wound.
For centuries, farmers in China have used weaver ants to protect citrus trees from pests.
It's one of the oldest known uses of *biological pest control*.
In some places, people even eat the larvae.
Builders, tailors, and guards, all in one tiny ant.
