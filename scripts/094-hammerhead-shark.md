Why does the hammerhead shark have such a *weird head*?
That wide, flat head is called a *cephalofoil*.
And it's packed with sensors that can detect the tiny *electrical signals* of living creatures.
It sweeps it over the sand like a *metal detector*, finding stingrays buried beneath the seafloor. [southern stingray | Hypanus americanus]
Then it pins them down with its head, and eats them.
It doesn't seem to mind the venomous spines either.
One great hammerhead was found with dozens of *stingray barbs* stuck in its mouth.
Its eyes sit at the ends of the hammer, giving it almost *360 degrees* of vision.
The head may also help it turn quickly, like a *wing*.
Great hammerheads can grow up to *six meters* long.
And surprisingly, they sometimes swim tilted on their *sides*, which helps save energy.
One of the ocean's strangest designs, and one of its best hunters.
