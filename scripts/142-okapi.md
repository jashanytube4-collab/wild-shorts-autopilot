This animal looks like part *zebra*, but it's actually related to the *giraffe*.
Meet the *okapi*, from the rainforests of the *Congo*.
It was so hard to find that Western scientists didn't describe it until *1901*.
Some called it the *African unicorn*.
Those striped legs probably help it hide in the dappled light of the forest.
They may also help calves follow their mothers.
Its tongue is so long it can lick its own *eyelids* and *ears*.
Okapis are shy and solitary, and they mark their territory with scent glands on their feet.
They can also communicate using deep sounds too low for humans to hear, called *infrasound*.
Babies spend their first weeks hidden in a nest, and they barely *poop*, which may help them avoid predators.
Okapis are now *endangered*, threatened by mining, logging and hunting.
