title: Why Foxes Jump Into Snow Headfirst
subject: red fox | Vulpes vulpes

Why do foxes dive *headfirst* into snow?
It's called *mousing*.
In winter, a *red fox* can hear a mouse moving under a meter of snow.
It stops, tilts its head, listens carefully, then leaps high and pounces straight down.
But scientists noticed something strange.
Foxes in one study jumped mostly in a *north-east* direction.
And when they did, their success rate was about *73 percent*.
In other directions, it dropped to around *18 percent*.
One idea is that foxes may use Earth's *magnetic field* to judge the distance to their prey, like a built-in rangefinder.
It's still unproven, but it's a fascinating possibility.
Red foxes live on almost every continent, more widespread than any other wild carnivore.
They're also clever city dwellers, living in *London*, Tokyo and New York.
And foxes store extra food in hidden spots, remembering where they buried it.
