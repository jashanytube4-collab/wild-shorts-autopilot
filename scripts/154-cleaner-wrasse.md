This tiny fish might recognize itself in a *mirror*.
Meet the *cleaner wrasse*.
In 2019, scientists put a colored mark on these fish where they could only see it in a mirror.
The fish looked at their reflection, then tried to *scrape* the mark off on the sand.
That's a test usually passed by great apes, dolphins and elephants.
Cleaner wrasses run *cleaning stations* on coral reefs.
Big fish, even sharks and groupers, line up to have parasites eaten off their bodies.
They open their mouths and gills, and let the tiny fish swim *inside*.
And no one eats the cleaner.
But sometimes cleaners *cheat*, biting off healthy mucus instead.
When other fish are watching, cleaners behave better, to protect their *reputation*.
And if a cleaner cheats, the client fish might chase it away.
Small fish, big brain, good *manners*.
