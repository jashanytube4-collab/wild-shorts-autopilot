This isn't a leaf. It's an *insect*.
Meet the *leaf insect*.
Its body is flat and green, with veins and even little *brown spots*, just like a real leaf.
Some have edges that look *chewed*, like a leaf nibbled by caterpillars.
When it walks, it sways back and forth, like a leaf in the *wind*.
Leaf insects live in Southeast Asia, Australia and the Pacific islands.
They eat leaves, which is a little ironic.
Their eggs look like *seeds*, and some may even be carried away by ants.
Many females can lay eggs without a male.
Leaf insects have been around for at least *47 million years*.
Scientists found a fossil leaf insect that looked almost identical to modern ones.
That means this disguise has worked for a very long time.
The perfect camouflage, perfected over millions of years.
