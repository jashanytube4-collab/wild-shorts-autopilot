In the 1970s, chimpanzees fought a *four-year war*.
It happened in Gombe, Tanzania, where *Jane Goodall* had been studying them for years.
One group of chimps split in two.
And the larger group began hunting the smaller one.
Patrols of males crossed into the other territory and attacked lone chimps, *one by one*.
By the end, the smaller group was *wiped out*.
Goodall later wrote that it changed how she saw them forever.
Chimpanzees are our closest living relatives, sharing about *98 percent* of our DNA.
They make tools, use sticks to fish for termites, and crack nuts with *stones*.
They hug, kiss, and *comfort* each other after fights.
But they're also incredibly strong, and can be *violent*.
They hunt monkeys in coordinated teams. [red colobus | Piliocolobus tephrosceles]
In many ways, chimps show both the best and the worst of us.
