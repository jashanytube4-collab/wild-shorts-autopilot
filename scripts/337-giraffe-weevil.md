This beetle has a neck like a *giraffe*.
Meet the *giraffe weevil*, from Madagascar.
Males have extremely long necks, about two to three times longer than females'.
They use them to *fight* other males for a mate.
The two males swing their necks at each other, like a duel.
Females have shorter necks, which they use to build nests.
A female rolls a leaf into a tube, and lays a single egg inside.
Then she cuts it loose from the tree.
When the larva hatches, it eats its way out of its leafy home.
The giraffe weevil's body is bright red, a warning to predators.
It's only around *2.5 centimeters* long.
And like many animals in Madagascar, it lives nowhere else on Earth.
A tiny beetle, built like a construction crane.
