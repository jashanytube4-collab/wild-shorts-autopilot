This is why you should *never touch* this creature.
An electric eel can fire up to *860 volts*, over *seven times* an American wall socket.
And here's the twist: it isn't even an eel.
It's a *knifefish*, more closely related to *catfish* than to true eels.
About *80 percent* of its body is electric organ, packed with thousands of cells called *electrocytes*.
Each one works like a *tiny battery*, and they all fire at once.
In the muddy rivers of the *Amazon*, weak pulses work like *radar*. [~amazon river]
Strong pulses make hidden prey *twitch*, giving away exactly where it's hiding.
When it feels threatened, it does something even scarier.
It *leaps out* of the water and presses its chin against the attacker, sending the shock *straight through* its body.
In 1800, explorer *Alexander von Humboldt* wrote that eels attacked *horses* this way. [horse | Equus caballus]
For two centuries, scientists doubted him, until a *2016 experiment* proved him right.
