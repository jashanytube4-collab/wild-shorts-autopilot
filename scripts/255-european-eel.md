For thousands of years, nobody knew how eels were *born*.
Even *Aristotle* thought they appeared from the mud.
Young *Sigmund Freud* spent weeks cutting open eels looking for their reproductive organs, and failed.
Here's what we know now.
European eels are thought to be born in the *Sargasso Sea*, in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean.
As tiny, see-through larvae shaped like *leaves*, they drift with the currents to Europe.
That journey can take a year or more.
They swim up rivers, and live in ponds and streams for decades.
Then, as adults, they swim back thousands of kilometers to the Sargasso Sea to spawn, and die.
But here's the crazy part: no one has ever seen European eels mating in the wild.
Scientists have tracked eels heading in that direction, but the final step remains a *mystery*.
Today, European eels are *critically endangered*.
