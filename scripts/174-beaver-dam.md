subject: North American beaver | Castor canadensis
mood: curious

The largest beaver dam ever found can be seen from *space*.
It's in Canada's *Wood Buffalo National Park*, and it's around *850 meters* long.
That's more than twice the length of the Hoover Dam.
Beavers have been building it since at least the *1970s*.
Beavers are nature's engineers.
They cut down trees with their orange teeth, which are strengthened with *iron*.
That's why they're orange.
Their teeth never stop growing, so chewing wood keeps them in shape.
Beavers build dams to create ponds, then build a lodge in the middle, with underwater *entrances*.
That keeps predators out.
Their ponds create wetlands that help fish, birds, frogs and many other animals.
Beavers can stay underwater for around *15 minutes*.
And they slap their flat tails on the water to warn others of danger.
The animal that reshapes rivers.
