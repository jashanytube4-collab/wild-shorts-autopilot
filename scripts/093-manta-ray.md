This fish might recognize itself in a *mirror*.
Meet the *manta ray*.
It has the biggest brain of any fish, relative to its body size.
In one experiment, manta rays in an aquarium acted strangely in front of a mirror, repeatedly checking themselves out.
Scientists think it could be a sign of *self-awareness*.
Giant mantas can reach around *seven meters* from wingtip to wingtip.
Despite their size, they feed on tiny *plankton*.
They do slow backflips, somersaulting through clouds of food.
And sometimes they leap high out of the water, landing with a huge *splash*.
Nobody knows exactly why.
Mantas visit *cleaning stations* on reefs, where small fish pick parasites off their skin.
They even seem to remember which stations they like best.
A giant, graceful, and possibly self-aware *fish*.
