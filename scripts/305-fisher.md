This animal is one of the only predators that regularly hunts *porcupines*. [North American porcupine | Erethizon dorsatum]
Meet the *fisher*, a member of the weasel family from the forests of North America.
Despite its name, it rarely eats fish.
To kill a porcupine, the fisher avoids the quills.
It attacks the face again and again, until the porcupine is exhausted.
Then it flips it over and attacks its soft, unprotected *belly*.
Fishers are excellent climbers.
They can rotate their back feet, so they can climb down trees *head first*.
They can travel long distances through forests, often over *ten kilometers* a day.
Fishers were wiped out from many areas, trapped for their fur.
But they've been reintroduced in places like Washington and Pennsylvania.
And some now live on the edges of suburbs.
A small, fierce forest hunter.
