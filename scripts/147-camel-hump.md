title: What's Really Inside A Camel's Hump
subject: dromedary camel | Camelus dromedarius
mood: curious

A camel's hump isn't full of *water*.
It's full of *fat*.
When food is scarce, camels can break down that fat for energy.
If a camel goes too long without eating, the hump can *flop* to one side.
So how do camels survive the desert?
They can go many days without drinking, and lose a large share of their body water without dying.
When they find water, they can drink over *100 liters* in about *ten minutes*.
Their blood cells are oval-shaped, so they can keep flowing even when the camel is dehydrated.
Camels have three eyelids and two rows of long eyelashes to keep sand out.
They can even close their *nostrils* in sandstorms.
Their thick lips let them eat thorny plants that would shred other animals' mouths.
And their body temperature rises during the day, so they sweat less and *save water*.
The ultimate desert machine.
