title: The Snake That Looks Like A Leaf
subject: emerald tree boa | Corallus caninus
mood: curious

This snake spends its life coiled on a branch, looking like a bunch of *leaves*.
Meet the *emerald tree boa*, from the Amazon rainforest.
It's bright green, with white zig-zag stripes along its back.
It drapes its coils over a branch, with its head resting in the middle.
At night, it hangs down and waits for birds or bats to pass. [bat | Chiroptera]
It has some of the longest front teeth of any non-venomous snake, perfect for gripping *feathers*.
Along its lips are heat-sensing pits, letting it see the warmth of its prey in the dark.
Here's the strange part: babies aren't green.
They're born *red*, orange or yellow.
They turn green as they grow, usually within their first year.
It looks almost identical to the green tree python of Australia and New Guinea, but they're not closely related. [green tree python | Morelia viridis]
They evolved the same look separately, an example of *convergent evolution*.
