Does the black widow really eat her *mate*?
Sometimes, but not as often as people think.
In the wild, many males escape after mating.
The *black widow* is famous for the red hourglass on its belly.
Its venom is about *15 times* stronger than a rattlesnake's, drop for drop.
But it only injects a tiny amount.
So bites are painful, but rarely deadly to healthy adults, and an antivenom exists.
Only the females are dangerous. Males are much smaller and their bites are weak.
Black widow silk is among the strongest of all spider silks.
During World War Two, it was used for the crosshairs in gun sights.
Black widows spin messy, tangled webs in dark, quiet places: woodpiles, sheds, and garages.
And they're actually shy. They'd rather hide than bite.
So shake out your gloves before working in the garden.
