title: Why Betta Fish Are Called Fighting Fish
subject: Siamese fighting fish | Betta splendens

Betta fish are called *fighting fish* for a reason.
Males are extremely territorial.
When two meet, they flare their fins and gills, and attack each other.
In Thailand, people have bred them for fighting for hundreds of years.
In the wild, bettas live in shallow rice paddies, ponds and slow streams.
These waters can be warm and low in *oxygen*.
So bettas have a special organ, called a *labyrinth*, that lets them breathe *air* from the surface.
Male bettas build nests of *bubbles* at the water's surface.
After mating, the male collects the eggs in his mouth, and places them in the bubble nest.
Then he guards them, and catches any that fall out.
Wild bettas are often dull green or brown.
The bright colors and long fins of pet bettas come from years of selective *breeding*.
Small fish, big personality.
