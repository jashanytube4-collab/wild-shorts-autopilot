This giant fish has to come up for *air*, or it will drown.
Meet the *arapaima*, one of the largest freshwater fish in the world.
It can grow up to around *three meters* long, and weigh over *200 kilograms*.
It lives in the Amazon basin, where the water can be low in oxygen.
So it gulps air at the surface, every *five to fifteen minutes*.
And it has armor-like scales that are almost *piranha-proof*. [red-bellied piranha | Pygocentrus nattereri]
Scientists found the scales have a hard outer layer, and flexible layers underneath, like a natural bulletproof vest.
Engineers are studying them for *body armor*.
Its tongue has bony teeth on it, and locals once used it as a kind of *sandpaper*.
Male arapaimas guard their babies, which swim close to their heads.
Overfishing made them rare in many places.
But in some Brazilian communities, local management has helped numbers recover *dramatically*.
