title: How A Rattlesnake's Rattle Works
subject: western diamondback rattlesnake | Crotalus atrox
mood: danger
tags: rattlesnake, snakes, usa, venomous snakes, desert animals
---
A rattlesnake's rattle can shake around *50 times* every second.
It's made of interlocking rings of *keratin*, the same material as your fingernails.
Every time the snake sheds its skin, it adds a new ring.
But you can't tell its age by counting them, because rings often *break off*.
Scientists discovered a sneaky trick.
When a threat gets closer, the rattlesnake suddenly speeds up its rattle, jumping to a higher *frequency*.
That makes people think it's closer than it really is, helping keep them at a *safe distance*.
The *western diamondback* is one of the biggest rattlesnakes, reaching over *two meters*.
It has heat-sensing pits on its face, so it can strike in total darkness.
Baby rattlesnakes are born with venom and can bite right away.
Even a dead rattlesnake can still bite by reflex for a while.
So please, keep your distance.
