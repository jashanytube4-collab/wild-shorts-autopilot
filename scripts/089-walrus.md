title: Why Walruses Have Giant Tusks
subject: walrus | Odobenus rosmarus
mood: curious
tags: walrus, arctic animals, tusks, ocean, marine mammals
---
A walrus uses its tusks to *climb*.
Its scientific name roughly means *tooth-walking sea horse*.
Those tusks are actually long *canine teeth* that can grow up to around *a meter*.
Walruses hook them onto the ice to haul their huge bodies out of the water.
Males also use them to fight, and the biggest tusks usually belong to the most dominant males.
A large male can weigh over *1,500 kilograms*.
They find food on the dark seafloor using around *400 to 700* sensitive whiskers.
Then they *suck* clams out of their shells, like a vacuum cleaner.
A walrus can eat thousands of clams in one meal.
When they're cold, their skin turns *pale*.
After warming up in the sun, they turn *pink*.
Walruses can also inflate air sacs in their neck to float upright while they *sleep*.
Basically, they have a built-in *life jacket*.
