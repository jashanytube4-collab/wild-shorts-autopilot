The deadliest hunter on Earth isn't a lion or a shark.
It might be the *dragonfly*.
Some studies show dragonflies catch up to *95 percent* of the prey they chase.
How? They don't just chase. They *predict*.
A dragonfly calculates where its prey is going, and flies to intercept it.
Its huge eyes are made of up to around *30,000* tiny lenses, giving it nearly *360-degree* vision.
Each of its four wings moves on its own, so it can hover, fly backwards, or turn on a dime.
Some can reach speeds of around *50 kilometers per hour*.
Dragonflies have been around for over *300 million years*.
Their ancient relatives had wingspans of around *70 centimeters*, the size of a hawk.
And most of a dragonfly's life is spent *underwater*, as a nymph that hunts tadpoles and even small fish.
Some species migrate thousands of kilometers.
Small, fast, and almost impossible to escape.
