This insect can walk on *water*.
Meet the *water strider*.
Its long legs are covered in thousands of tiny *hairs*.
They trap air, making the legs waterproof.
So instead of breaking through the surface, they press down and make small dimples.
The water's surface tension holds them up, like a stretchy *skin*.
Water striders can glide across the surface at around *one meter per second*.
They can even jump off the water.
They sense ripples from insects that fall in, and race over to eat them.
They also communicate by tapping the water to make *ripples*.
Some species live on the open ocean, far from land.
These ocean striders lay eggs on floating objects, even plastic trash.
Engineers have built tiny robots inspired by water striders that can also jump off water.
A tiny insect, defying physics.
