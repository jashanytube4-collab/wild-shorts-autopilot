This octopus carries its *house* around with it.
Meet the *coconut octopus*.
It collects empty *coconut shells* and seashells from the seafloor.
When it needs to hide, it climbs inside and pulls the two halves closed around it.
But here's the amazing part: it carries the shells with it, for later.
It stacks them under its body, and walks along the seafloor on two arms, like it's on *stilts*.
Scientists call this one of the first known examples of *tool use* in an invertebrate.
Because it's carrying something now, for a future use.
Coconut octopuses also hide by burying themselves in sand, with only their eyes peeking out.
When it walks on two arms, it can even disguise itself as a rolling ball of algae.
They live in the warm waters of the western Pacific and Indian Ocean.
A tiny octopus, with its own portable *bunker*.
