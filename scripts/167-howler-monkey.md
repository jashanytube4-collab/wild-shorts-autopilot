The loudest land animal in the Americas is a *monkey*.
Meet the *howler monkey*.
Its call can be heard up to around *five kilometers* away through the rainforest.
How? It has a large, hollow bone in its throat, called the *hyoid*, that amplifies its voice.
Groups howl together, especially at *dawn*, to tell other groups where they are.
It's a way to avoid fights without ever getting close.
But scientists found a strange trade-off.
Males with the *loudest* voices and biggest throat bones tend to have *smaller* testicles.
Quieter males have bigger ones.
Howler monkeys eat mostly leaves, which give little energy.
So they spend most of their day *resting*, sometimes over *15 hours*.
Their long tails can grab branches like a fifth *hand*.
In Central and South America, they're the soundtrack of the rainforest.
