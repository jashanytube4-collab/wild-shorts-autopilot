In the Amazon, hundreds of parrots gather every morning to eat *clay*.
These clay licks are riverbanks where *scarlet macaws*, and many other parrots, come to feed.
It's one of the most colorful sights in nature. [red-and-green macaw | Ara chloropterus]
But why eat dirt?
For a long time, scientists thought clay helped them deal with toxins in seeds they eat.
Newer research suggests something else: *salt*.
Inland rainforests are low in sodium, and the clay provides it.
Scarlet macaws are some of the most colorful birds on Earth.
They can live for over *50 years*, and they mate for life.
Their beaks are strong enough to crack open hard nuts.
They're also very social, flying in pairs and families, calling loudly.
Sadly, they've been heavily captured for the pet trade.
A colorful breakfast, by the riverbank.
