This butterfly has *owl eyes* on its wings.
Meet the *owl butterfly*, from the rainforests of Central and South America.
On the underside of its wings are huge, round spots that look like the eyes of an *owl*.
For years, people thought they scared off predators by looking like an owl's face.
But scientists now think the eyespots might work differently.
Birds may simply avoid things that look like big, staring eyes.
Or the spots may draw attacks away from the butterfly's head.
Owl butterflies are big, with wings spanning up to *15 centimeters*.
They fly mostly at dusk, when the light is low.
They feed on rotting fruit, and some even drink tree sap.
Their caterpillars can be pests on banana plantations.
They're so big that they can be seen fluttering through the forest from far away.
Big eyes, big wings, and big mystery.
