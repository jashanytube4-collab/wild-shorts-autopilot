title: How Thousands Of Birds Move As One
subject: common starling | Sturnus vulgaris
mood: epic
tags: starling murmuration, birds, flocking, nature, wildlife
---
Thousands of birds swirling in the sky, moving as if they were *one*.
This is a *murmuration* of *starlings*.
Some flocks can contain hundreds of thousands of birds.
So how do they avoid crashing?
Scientists found each starling keeps track of around *seven* of its closest neighbors.
When one changes direction, the change ripples across the whole flock in a fraction of a second.
That creates those amazing shapes in the sky.
Murmurations may help protect starlings from predators like *falcons*. [peregrine falcon | Falco peregrinus]
It's hard to target one bird in a swirling cloud.
They're also probably a way to share information about roosting spots.
Starlings were brought to North America in the 1890s.
Supposedly, someone wanted to introduce every bird mentioned in Shakespeare.
Today, there are millions of them across the continent.
A living cloud, painting the sky.
