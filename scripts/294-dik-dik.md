This antelope is about the size of a *house cat*.
Meet the *dik-dik*, one of the smallest antelopes in the world.
It weighs only around *five kilograms*.
Its name comes from the sound it makes when it's alarmed: "*dik-dik*!"
That alarm also warns other animals nearby.
Its long, flexible nose works like a built-in *air conditioner*.
Blood flows through it and cools off before going back to the body.
Dik-diks live in the dry bushland of East Africa.
They get most of their water from the plants they eat, and rarely need to drink.
Dik-diks are known for pairing up for life with one partner.
They mark their territory with dung piles, and with sticky tears from glands near their eyes.
When danger comes, they zig-zag at up to around *40 kilometers per hour*.
Small, loyal, and always on alert.
