title: How Chameleons Really Change Color
subject: panther chameleon | Furcifer pardalis
mood: curious
tags: chameleon, color change, madagascar, reptiles, weird animals
---
Chameleons don't change color to *hide*.
Not mainly, anyway.
Most of the time, they change color to send *messages*, and to control their *temperature*.
Under their skin are cells filled with tiny *crystals*.
When a chameleon relaxes or excites those cells, the spacing between the crystals changes.
That changes how light reflects, shifting the color.
A calm *panther chameleon* is often green.
An angry male can flash bright red, orange and *yellow*.
And their tongues are just as amazing.
A chameleon's tongue can be longer than its *body*, shooting out to catch insects in a fraction of a second.
Its eyes move *independently*, so it can look in two directions at once.
When it spots prey, both eyes lock onto it to judge the distance.
Then the tongue fires faster than you can blink.
Many chameleon species live only on the island of *Madagascar*.
