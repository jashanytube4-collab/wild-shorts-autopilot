title: The Ants That Grow Their Own Food
subject: leafcutter ant | Atta cephalotes
mood: curious
tags: leafcutter ants, ants, farming, insects, rainforest
---
These ants were *farming* millions of years before humans.
Meet the *leafcutter ants*.
They cut pieces of leaves and carry them home, often many times their own weight.
But they don't eat the leaves.
They feed them to a *fungus* that they grow underground in special gardens.
The ants eat the fungus.
And they look after it like expert farmers.
They weed out bad mold, and some even carry *bacteria* on their bodies that work like natural pesticides.
A single colony can have over *five million* ants.
Their nests can stretch dozens of meters underground with hundreds of chambers.
Some ants have different jobs: cutters, carriers, gardeners, and big *soldiers* that guard the trails.
Tiny workers even ride on the leaves to protect the carriers from parasitic flies.
A whole city, with its own farms, run without a single *boss*.
