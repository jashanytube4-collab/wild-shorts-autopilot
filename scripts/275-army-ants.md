These ants build *bridges* and *houses* out of their own bodies.
Meet the *army ants* of Central and South America.
A single colony can contain *hundreds of thousands* of ants.
They don't have a permanent nest.
Instead, they form a living home called a *bivouac*, made of ants linking legs.
The queen and young are kept safe inside.
Every day, they march out in huge swarms, hunting insects, spiders and other small animals.
When they reach a gap, some ants link together into a living *bridge*, and the rest march over them.
Birds follow the swarms, catching the insects trying to escape.
Army ants are almost *blind*.
They follow chemical trails laid by the ants in front.
Sometimes, if the trail loops back on itself, they get stuck marching in a circle until they die, called an *ant mill*.
A living army, always on the move.
