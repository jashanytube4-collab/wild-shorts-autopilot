title: How Fireflies Make Light
subject: common eastern firefly | Photinus pyralis
mood: curious

Fireflies make light without making *heat*.
Inside their bellies, a chemical reaction turns almost all of its energy into *light*.
A normal light bulb wastes most of its energy as heat.
Fireflies are actually *beetles*.
And they flash to talk to each other.
Each species has its own flash *pattern*, like a secret code.
Males fly around flashing, and females on the ground flash back if they're interested.
But some females have a deadly trick.
*Femme fatale* fireflies copy the flash pattern of another species. [Photuris | Photuris]
When males of that species come close, she *eats* them.
In places like the Great Smoky Mountains, thousands of fireflies flash in *sync*.
People travel from around the world to watch it.
Sadly, firefly numbers are falling, partly because of *light pollution*, which drowns out their signals.
Turning off outdoor lights can help them find each other.
