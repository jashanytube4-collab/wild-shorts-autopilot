title: Snakes That Can Fly
subject: paradise tree snake | Chrysopelea paradisi
mood: curious

Yes, some snakes can *fly*.
Meet the *paradise tree snake* from Southeast Asia.
It climbs to the top of a tree, slides to the end of a branch, and *launches* itself into the air.
Mid-flight, it flattens its body into a shape like a *ribbon*, almost doubling its width.
Then it wiggles from side to side, as if swimming through the air.
That wiggle keeps it stable and helps it *glide*.
These snakes can travel up to around *100 meters* in a single glide.
And they can even *steer*.
Scientists tested flying snakes in a tall indoor tower using high-speed cameras.
They found the snake's flattened body works almost like an *airplane wing*, generating lift.
Flying snakes are mildly venomous, but harmless to humans.
They mostly use their flights to escape predators, or travel between trees.
So if you're ever in a jungle, remember to look *up*.
