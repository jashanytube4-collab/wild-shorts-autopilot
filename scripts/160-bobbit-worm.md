This worm can grow *three meters* long, and it hides in the sand.
Meet the *bobbit worm*.
Most of its body stays buried in the seafloor.
Only its head pokes out, with five antennae that sense movement.
When a fish swims too close, it shoots up with incredible speed and snaps its jaws shut.
The strike can be so fast and powerful that it can slice a fish in half.
Then it drags its prey down into the sand.
Its jaws, which can open like scissors, are covered in sharp edges.
It can also inject a toxin to stun prey.
Bobbit worms sometimes end up in aquariums by accident, hiding in rocks.
One was discovered in an English aquarium after it had been secretly eating fish for years.
It was around *1.2 meters* long.
Scientists have also found burrows from similar worms in fossils over *20 million* years old.
