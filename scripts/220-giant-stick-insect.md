This isn't a twig. It's an *insect*.
Stick insects are some of the best disguised animals on Earth.
Some species can grow over *60 centimeters* long, with legs stretched out.
They sway back and forth, like a twig moving in the *wind*.
The *spiny leaf insect* from Australia has a strange trick.
Its eggs look just like seeds, and they have a tasty knob that *ants* love. [ant | Formicidae]
Ants carry the eggs into their nests, where they're safe from parasites.
When the babies hatch, they look and run like *ants*, to escape the nest.
And some female stick insects don't need males at all.
They can lay eggs that hatch into *clones* of themselves.
One species, the Lord Howe Island stick insect, was thought to be *extinct*.
Then, in 2001, a few were found clinging to a single bush on a rocky sea stack.
Today, thousands have been bred in zoos.
Nature's best hide-and-seek player.
