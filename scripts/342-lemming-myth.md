Do lemmings really jump off cliffs in mass suicide?
*No*.
That myth became famous thanks to a 1958 *Disney* documentary called *White Wilderness*.
It showed lemmings leaping into the sea.
But it was staged.
Filmmakers reportedly brought lemmings to Canada and pushed them off a cliff for the cameras.
In reality, lemming populations *boom* every few years, then crash.
When numbers get too high, they spread out to find new homes.
Sometimes, while crossing rivers or lakes, some *drown*.
But they're not trying to.
The *Norway lemming* is actually tough and bold.
When threatened, it often stands its ground, squeaking and hissing, instead of running away.
Their population cycles affect the whole Arctic, because so many predators eat them. [snowy owl | Bubo scandiacus]
Lots of lemmings means lots of owls, foxes and weasels.
A tiny rodent, wrongly famous for something it never did.
