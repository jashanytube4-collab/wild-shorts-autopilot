title: The Termite-Eating Marsupial With Stripes
subject: numbat | Myrmecobius fasciatus
mood: curious
tags: numbat, australia, marsupial, endangered, termites
---
This striped marsupial eats around *20,000 termites* every day.
Meet the *numbat*, from Western Australia.
It's about the size of a squirrel, with white stripes across its back and a bushy *tail*.
Unlike most marsupials, it's active during the *day*.
That's when termites are moving near the surface.
It uses its long, sticky tongue, around *ten centimeters* long, to lick them up.
Numbats don't have a pouch.
Babies cling to their mother's belly, holding onto her fur.
They have more teeth than almost any other land mammal, around *50*, but they barely use them.
Numbats once lived across southern Australia.
But foxes and feral cats brought them close to *extinction*.
Today, conservation programs protect them in fenced reserves.
It's the official animal emblem of *Western Australia*.
