title: Why Woodpeckers Don't Get Headaches
subject: pileated woodpecker | Dryocopus pileatus
mood: curious
tags: woodpecker, birds, headache, animal facts, forest
---
A woodpecker can hit wood around *20 times every second*.
So why doesn't it get a *headache*?
Scientists used to think its skull worked like a *shock absorber*.
But a 2022 study found something surprising.
The skull is actually stiff, like a *hammer*.
Their small brains are simply less likely to be injured by those forces.
And a stiffer head drives the beak deeper into the wood.
Woodpeckers also have a crazy *tongue*.
In some species, it's so long that it wraps around the back of the skull, all the way to the front of the head.
It has tiny barbs and sticky saliva to pull insects out of holes.
The *pileated woodpecker* is one of the biggest in North America, and it digs rectangular holes so deep that they can *break* small trees in half.
Its drumming isn't just for food.
It's also how it *talks*, claiming territory and calling for a mate.
