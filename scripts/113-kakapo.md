This is the world's *heaviest parrot*, and it can't fly.
Meet the *kakapo* from New Zealand.
It can weigh up to around *four kilograms*.
Instead of flying, it *climbs* trees, and uses its wings like a parachute to flutter down.
It's *nocturnal*, it smells like sweet honey, and it can live over *60 years*.
When predators like hawks came near, its strategy was simple: *freeze* and stay perfectly still.
That worked against birds that hunted by sight.
But when humans brought cats, rats and stoats, which hunt by *smell*, it was a disaster. [stoat | Mustela erminea]
By the 1990s, only around *50* kakapos were left.
Today, every single one has a name, and is closely monitored by conservationists.
Thanks to their work, the population has now passed *200*.
Males attract females by inflating their chests and making deep *booming* sounds that travel kilometers.
A chunky, honey-scented miracle of conservation.
