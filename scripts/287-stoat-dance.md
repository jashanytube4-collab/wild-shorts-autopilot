This little animal *dances* to hypnotize its prey.
Meet the *stoat*.
When it spots a rabbit, it starts leaping, twisting and flipping in a wild, crazy *dance*. [European rabbit | Oryctolagus cuniculus]
The rabbit seems frozen, staring at the strange show.
Then, the stoat gets closer and closer, and suddenly *strikes*.
Rabbits can be around *ten times* the stoat's size.
Scientists still debate whether the dance is a deliberate trick, or caused by a parasite that irritates the stoat's brain.
In winter, stoats in snowy places turn completely *white*, except for the tip of their tail, which stays black.
In this white coat, they're called *ermine*.
Kings and queens once wore ermine fur on their royal robes.
Stoats are fierce hunters, killing prey with a bite to the back of the neck.
They're found across Europe, Asia and North America.
Small, fast, and a little bit hypnotic.
