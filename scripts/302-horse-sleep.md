Can horses really sleep *standing up*?
Yes, and no.
Horses have a special system in their legs, called the *stay apparatus*.
It locks their joints, letting them doze off without falling over.
But for deep sleep, called REM sleep, horses need to *lie down*.
They only need around *two to three hours* of sleep a day, often in short naps.
Wild horses take turns, with one horse staying awake to keep *watch*.
Horses have the largest eyes of any land mammal.
Their eyes are on the sides of their heads, giving them almost *350-degree* vision.
But they have blind spots directly in front of their nose and behind them.
That's why you should never walk directly behind a horse.
Horses can't vomit, because of a strong muscle where the food pipe meets the stomach.
And they can recognize human *facial expressions*, like happiness or anger.
Powerful, sensitive, and always half awake.
