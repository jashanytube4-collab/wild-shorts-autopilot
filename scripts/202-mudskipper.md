This fish spends most of its time *out of the water*.
Meet the *mudskipper*.
It walks across mudflats using its strong front fins like *crutches*.
It can even skip and leap, sometimes over half a meter.
But how does a fish breathe on land?
It carries water in its big gill chambers, like a scuba diver carrying *air*.
And it can absorb oxygen through its skin and the lining of its mouth.
To stay moist, it rolls around in the mud.
Its eyes sit on top of its head, and can pop up like *periscopes*.
When they dry out, it blinks by pulling them down into its head.
Males build mud *burrows*, and leap high into the air to attract females.
They even store air inside their burrows for their eggs.
Scientists study mudskippers to understand how ancient fish first moved onto *land*.
