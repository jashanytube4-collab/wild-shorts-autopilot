In Florida, when it gets cold enough, *iguanas fall from trees*.
Weather forecasters even warn about it.
*Green iguanas* sleep high in trees.
When the temperature drops below around *ten degrees Celsius*, they slow down.
Below around *five degrees*, they can't move at all, and they let go of the branch.
They land on the ground looking *dead*.
But most aren't. When it warms up, they wake up and walk away.
Green iguanas aren't native to Florida. They came from Central and South America, probably through the pet trade.
Today, they're an *invasive* species.
They can grow over *1.5 meters* long, including the tail.
They can also drop their tails to escape predators, and grow them back.
And on top of their heads, they have a *third eye* that senses light and shadows.
Raining iguanas, only in Florida.
