Are piranhas really as deadly as the movies say?
Mostly, *no*.
The *red-bellied piranha* lives in the rivers of South America, and yes, its teeth are razor sharp.
They're shaped like triangles that interlock, perfect for slicing chunks of *flesh*.
For its size, it has one of the strongest bites of any fish.
But piranhas usually eat insects, fish, plants and *carrion*.
And they don't swim in schools to hunt. Scientists think they group up for *safety*, to avoid being eaten themselves.
So where did the myth come from?
In 1913, *Theodore Roosevelt* visited Brazil.
Locals blocked off a section of river, starved the piranhas, then pushed a *cow* into the water.
The fish stripped it in minutes.
Roosevelt wrote about it, and the legend was born.
They can bite, and occasionally they do, but a piranha would rather eat *your bait* than you.
