title: The Fish That Looks Like Seaweed
subject: leafy seadragon | Phycodurus eques
mood: curious

This isn't seaweed. It's a *fish*.
Meet the *leafy seadragon*, from the waters of southern Australia.
Its body is covered in leaf-shaped flaps that look exactly like drifting *seaweed*.
It doesn't use them to swim.
Instead, it moves using tiny, see-through fins on its neck and back, which are almost *invisible*.
So it seems to float along like a piece of kelp.
It's a close relative of the *seahorse*.
And just like seahorses, the *dads* carry the eggs.
The female lays up to around *250 eggs* onto the underside of the male's tail.
He carries them for weeks until they hatch.
Leafy seadragons have no teeth and no stomach.
They suck up tiny shrimp through their long, pipe-like snout.
It's the official marine emblem of the state of *South Australia*.
Nature's most beautiful disguise.
